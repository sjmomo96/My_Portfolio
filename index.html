Salman Jahan Momo

I am Salman Jahan Momo, a Lecturer in Mathematics and Science at an English-medium school in Thailand. I hold a BSc in Computer Science and Engineering from the University of Asia Pacific and an MSc in Marine Plastic Abatement from the Asian Institute of Technology. My background combines computer science, applied research, and teaching, with a focus on data-driven solutions to real-world challenges.

My interests include machine learning, medical image analysis, misinformation detection, data fusion, brain function, and computational approaches to human behaviour. I aim to develop reliable, interpretable methods for decision-making under uncertainty, with potential applications in public health, disease detection, and healthcare resource planning. My experience also includes environmental monitoring, GIS mapping of pollution hotspots, and automated plant disease diagnosis.

Ultimately, I hope to develop practical computational methods that support informed decisions when data are incomplete or uncertain.

Research

Selected publications.

Accepted5th International Conference on Innovations in Science, Engineering and Technology (ICISET) · 2026

Stylometric & Credibility-Aware Four-Way Human and AI-Generated Misinformation Classification

Natural Language ProcessingMachine LearningComputational LinguisticsAI-Generated Text
Read abstract

Large language models can generate fluent and persuasive fake content at scale, introducing new challenges for political misinformation detection. Fake news detection has traditionally focused on determining whether text is factual, while AI-based approaches have focused on identifying where text came from; this paper brings those tasks together. It addresses four-class classification on a custom dataset of political statements derived from LIAR, labelled Human-Real, Human-Fake, AI-Real, or AI-Fake. The proposed interpretable, feature-driven framework uses leakage-aware preprocessing, ablation across eight linguistic and stylometric feature families, chi-square feature selection, and credibility-aware metadata fusion across four classical machine-learning classifiers. The best text-only model achieves 60.95% test accuracy and a 0.5966 Macro F1 score. Adding credibility metadata improves performance to 76.08% test accuracy and a 0.7540 Macro F1 score. A controlled Logistic Regression comparison finds that metadata alone contributes a 14.54 percentage-point accuracy gain. The results suggest that source credibility is a key missing signal: text features help identify authorship, but are not sufficient to establish the factual veracity of political text.

AcceptedIEEE International Conference on Biomedical Engineering, Computer and Information Technology for Health (BECITHCON) · 2026

Confidence-Aware Referee Network for Multi-Source Fundus Image-Based Cataract Detection

Medical ImagingComputer VisionMachine LearningHealthcare AI
Read abstract

Cataract is a leading cause of preventable blindness, yet automated detection from fundus images is challenged by data heterogeneity, cross-split leakage, and uncertainty in single-model predictions. This paper proposes a Confidence-Aware Referee Network (CARN) for cataract detection using multi-source retinal fundus images. It combines 4,464 images from ODIR-5K, AMDNet23, and GitHub Retina, removing 19 cross-split leakage samples through duplicate detection with pHash. A six-step, source-aware preprocessing pipeline uses Ben Graham illumination normalization and CLAHE to reduce domain shift between imaging devices. EfficientNet-B2 and DenseNet121 are trained independently, achieving test accuracies of 97.41% and 95.58%, respectively. A Gradient Boosting classifier acts as a referee, resolving disagreements using model confidence scores and physics-derived image features. CARN achieves 98.17% overall test accuracy, 92.92% sensitivity, and 99.26% specificity. Ablation studies validate the contribution of each component.
